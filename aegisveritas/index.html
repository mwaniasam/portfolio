Two bidders. One owner.

A system that finds bidders in Kenyan public procurement that look independent but are linked to each other or to a debarred party, before the contract is signed. It reads open award, ownership and contact records, ranks supplier pairs by how likely they are to share an owner, and shows the evidence behind every flag. A person always decides.

01 · Problem

What the committee can't see

A debarred company rarely bids under its own name. It bids through a clean-looking company that shares a director, owner, phone number or address with it.

A name check passes that company, because there is no name to match. Two "competing" bidders can also belong to the same person, so the committee compares them, picks the cheaper one and signs, and the competition was never real. Kenya's tender documents already ask bidders to declare common control, but an evaluation committee sees one tender at a time, and the links are spread across tens of thousands of firms.

02 · System

What I built

A full system, not a notebook: a data pipeline over Kenya's public procurement records, a screening service, an evidence engine that retrains and gates itself, and an investigator-facing web app. It runs on a laptop-class machine with Docker Compose.

01

Bidder screening

Checks a supplier against sanctions and debarment lists, then follows owners and directors two steps out to find exposure a name check misses.

02

Review queue and evidence cards

A short list of supplier pairs for an investigator, each with its evidence, how strong that kind of evidence has proven, and what to check next.

03

Pre-award tender check

Takes the bidders on one tender and reports any links between them in seconds, the way Kenya's e-GP system would call it at bid opening.

04

Ownership path tracing

Draws the path from a supplier to the listed party on an investigation canvas.

05

Tamper-evident audit trail

Every screening, decision and tender check is appended to a SHA-256 hash chain. Changing any past entry breaks the chain, and the app can verify it.

06

Role-based access

Only investigator, auditor and admin roles see named pairs. Each institution sees only its own data, enforced by PostgreSQL row-level security.

03 · Method

My first approach failed

The proposal asked "is this one firm bad?" After the data was cleaned, only 18 confirmed Kenyan cases remained, and 11 of them had fewer than three recorded links.

No graph model beat a baseline that only looked at company size. The labels decide what is possible, so after a supervision meeting I changed the question to "are these two firms under one control?" For that question, the ownership details Kenyan awards must disclose give 12,441 labelled links. Kenya has no open ownership register to test hidden links against, so I also validated the method in Estonia, where both procurement data and beneficial ownership are public.

Three kinds of evidence, one probability

Candidate pairs are firms that win from the same buyer within 30 days, or that share a disclosed record. Each pair gets evidence from three channels, each a log-likelihood ratio, combined Fellegi-Sunter style so every part of the score stays explainable. A contact shared by many firms counts for less, and the behaviour model is capped so it can never outweigh an ID match.

How much each clue multiplies the odds that two firms share an owner

Behaviour model

LightGBM on 20 award-pattern features, trained on Kenyan and Estonian pairs, with isotonic calibration. Calibration error 0.010. Thirteen models were compared; none beat it.

Knows when to abstain

Silent on pairs where a firm has fewer than 3 awards, and on pairs unlike its training data. Refusing to judge is a result, not a failure.

Gated releases

A retrained model goes live only if it beats both the baseline and the current live model on a fixed holdout. The rule is in the code.

05 · What failed

Reported in full

Failed and withdrawn results sit in the results ledger next to the ones that passed. They are the reason the passing results are worth believing.

0.065

A model trained on Estonia alone failed in Kenya

On the Kenyan holdout it scored 0.065 and 0.058 (raw and normalised), no better than the 0.067 baseline.

Markets differ. Each country needs its own labels, so the live model learns from Kenyan data.
2×

An outside test on road-contractor registers missed its pass mark

The pre-registered KeRRA test found no signal at its primary endpoint, twice: with the pair model and with the evidence engine.

The system now refuses to judge pairs unlike its training data.
0.075

My own safety gate caught a weaker model running live

The engine-2 model used importance weighting that was never tested, and a step-shaped calibrator.

Engine-3 trains exactly the model that was tested, and scores 0.146.
0.87

A result I withdrew

An Estonia-to-Kenya AUC of 0.87 turned out to have read the synthetic sample market, not real data.

Withdrawn and replaced by the real-data runs above.
242× → 209.9×

Every number rerun after submission

The submitted figures came from a run on an older database that still held duplicate company records. After a clean rebuild, concealed pairs went from 3,976 to 3,916 and the P.O. Box ratio from 242 to 209.9 times. The submission is tagged in git, and every run is kept.

The conclusion is unchanged; the page reports the rerun.

Design

Seven data connectors implement one abstract connector; two risk models share one interface, so the graph model can be swapped for rules. The screening orchestrator depends on interfaces, not on Neo4j or PyTorch, so a baseline can replace the model with no other change.

Responsible by design

A person always decides, as Kenya's Data Protection Act requires. The API serves links by kind and date, never people's names. Firm names can be masked as stable codes for recordings. The system is not on the open internet, because its findings name firms that have not been found to do anything wrong. Invited evaluators reach it through Cloudflare Access, behind a one-time code and an app account.

08 · Limits and next

A reason to look, not an accusation

Known limitations

Hidden links in Kenya are not yet proven: there is no public register to check against. 75 of the top 100 Kenyan pairs are not confirmed links; some may be hidden links and some will be innocent. Pairs where a firm has fewer than 3 awards (770,624 candidates) are not scored. Suppliers are matched by normalised name, so two different firms with the same name would be treated as one, and entity resolution precision has not yet been measured on a hand-checked sample. A 9,000-firm stress test ran out of memory, so scoring moves to batches next.

Now and next

Invite-only access has been live since 28 October 2026. Expert evaluation is under way: practitioners complete guided tasks in the app and a questionnaire that includes the System Usability Scale, under ALU ethics approval SEPT26-BEL-070. Next is a pilot with an organisation that works with procurement authorities.

When the public pays for competition, it should get competition.
